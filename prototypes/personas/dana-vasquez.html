Why Northbridge
A multinational financial services company was chosen deliberately: Dana is simultaneously on the hook for DORA (EU), OSFI B-10/B-13 (Canada), and NYDFS/GLBA/US interagency guidance (US).

Goals

  • Maintain a single, real-time, audit-ready register of critical vendors — especially ICT and AI vendors — across all three jurisdictions.
  • Cut new-vendor assessment time from weeks to days without lowering the bar.
  • Move from point-in-time annual reviews to continuous vendor risk monitoring.
  • Walk into any regulatory exam or audit committee meeting able to prove the program is complete and current.
  • Be seen by the business as an enabler of vendor relationships, not a bottleneck.
  • Grow into a Director of Third-Party Risk role by proving she can scale the program without linearly scaling headcount.

  • 5:00 pm

Responsibilities

  • Owns the full vendor risk lifecycle: intake, due diligence, contract-stage risk input, ongoing monitoring, issue remediation, offboarding.
  • Maintains the critical-vendor register required under DORA (EU) and OSFI B-10 (Canada), and aligned with US interagency third-party risk guidance.
  • Coordinates across Legal, Procurement, Security/GRC, Privacy, and business-unit stakeholders.
  • Reports vendor risk metrics to risk committees, internal audit, and regulators.
  • Manages and mentors two analysts performing day-to-day assessments.

Pain points

  • Fragmented tooling — vendor data is split across spreadsheets, email threads, SharePoint, and a legacy GRC/ticketing tool with no continuous monitoring. No single source of truth.
  • Manual document review is slow — she and her analysts read 60–80 page SOC 2 reports and security questionnaires by hand, per vendor, per cycle.
  • Point-in-time blind spots — a vendor can pass its annual review and get breached five months later with no one noticing until it's a crisis.
  • Audit defensibility is hard-won — proving to DORA/OSFI/NYDFS auditors that the register is complete and correctly tiered means weeks of manual binder-building before every exam.
  • No clean answer on AI risk — nothing tells her, at intake, whether a vendor's embedded AI features would make them "high-risk" under the EU AI Act; it gets litigated ad hoc with Legal every time.
  • Inconsistent criticality definitions — DORA, OSFI B-10, and US interagency guidance each define "critical third party" slightly differently, making one clean internal taxonomy hard to maintain.
  • Perception problem — the business sees her team as slow and bureaucratic; she wants speed without compromising rigor.
  • Analyst time sink — Priya and Marcus spend most of their week on repetitive document review instead of the judgment calls she actually needs them making.

Motivations

  • Professional pride in running a program that would hold up under any regulator's scrutiny.
  • Wants her team seen as risk-enablers, not blockers.
  • Worried about being a single point of failure — if she's out, no one else has full visibility into vendor risk status.
  • Wants to scale the program's coverage without needing to scale headcount at the same rate.

Tools she uses today (pre-Tessera)

  • Excel/Google Sheets for the vendor inventory.
  • SharePoint for storing vendor documentation (SOC 2 reports, questionnaires, contracts).
  • Email for chasing vendors and internal stakeholders for evidence.
  • A legacy GRC/ticketing tool for workflow tracking — no continuous monitoring, no AI assistance.
  • Hand-built PowerPoint/Excel decks for board and audit committee reporting.

Compliance frameworks Dana is personally accountable for

  • DORA — EU operations: ICT third-party register, critical ICT provider oversight.
  • OSFI B-10 / B-13 — Canadian operations: third-party and technology/cyber risk management.
  • NYDFS 23 NYCRR 500, GLBA Safeguards Rule, US interagency TPRM guidance — US operations.
  • EU AI Act — screening vendors whose products embed AI.
  • SOC 2 Type II / ISO 27001 / ISO 42001 — the evidence artifacts she and her analysts review.

How Tessera helps Dana

  • One continuously updated vendor register, auto-tiered by criticality against DORA, OSFI, and US frameworks side by side.
  • AI-assisted review of SOC 2 reports, questionnaires, and ISO certificates that flags gaps and risk indicators instead of requiring a full manual read-through.
  • Continuous monitoring surfaces emerging risk (breach signals, rating changes, expiring certifications) between review cycles, not just at renewal.
  • Built-in EU AI Act screening logic pre-flags vendors whose AI features may warrant deeper review, before it becomes an ad hoc Legal escalation.
  • Audit-ready reporting mapped directly to DORA / OSFI / NYDFS requirements, cutting board and audit prep from weeks to hours.
  • Every AI-assisted finding carries a human-in-the-loop review step and an audit trail, so Dana can defend the program's decisions to auditors and regulators.
