Tech comfort
Moderate — comfortable with spreadsheets, questionnaires, and standard business tools; not yet reading raw security evidence independently.
Role on the team
Marcus is the team's volume and intake engine: he triages and assesses the long tail of standard, lower-risk vendors, and escalates anything complex to Priya or Dana.

Goals

  • Clear his intake queue without missing SLAs, even as the number of new vendor requests keeps growing.
  • Build enough technical fluency to move from generalist intake work into the kind of deep-dive assessments Priya handles.
  • Prove himself early in his career — be known as reliable, not as the reason an urgent vendor onboarding got delayed.

A day in the life

  • 8:30 am

    Opens his queue to find 15+ new vendor intake requests waiting.

  • 9:00 am

    Runs standard risk questionnaires for a batch of low-to-medium risk vendors.

  • 10:30 am

    Chases three vendors by email for missing attestations or incomplete questionnaire responses.

  • 11:15 am

    Procurement pings him: a vendor needs to be cleared today for a contract signing. He has to figure out fast whether this is routine enough to fast-track or whether it needs to go to Priya.

  • 1:00 pm

    Sits in on Dana's team stand-up.

  • 2–5 pm

    Manually re-keys vendor questionnaire answers from PDF submissions into the shared tracking spreadsheet, one vendor at a time.

Responsibilities

  • Triages incoming vendor intake requests and runs standard risk questionnaires.
  • Performs first-pass assessment of low-to-medium risk vendors.
  • Coordinates with Procurement and Legal during vendor onboarding.
  • Escalates complex, ambiguous, or potentially critical vendors to Priya or Dana.
  • Maintains the shared vendor tracking spreadsheet/register.

Pain points

  • Repetitive, low-judgment work — most of his day is manual data entry and questionnaire logistics, leaving little room to build the technical skills he actually wants.
  • Manual re-keying — retyping the same vendor answers out of PDF submissions into a spreadsheet is tedious and error-prone, and mistakes reflect on his credibility.
  • No clean way to know what's "routine" — without asking Priya or Dana, he often can't tell whether a vendor that looks standard is secretly more complex, so he either escalates too much or too little.
  • Invisible SLA math — he has no clear at-a-glance view of what's overdue across his queue; tracking deadlines is manual.
  • Pressure without visibility — he feels the weight of not wanting to block the business's urgent requests, but has little control over how fast the process can actually move.
  • Unclear growth path — worried he'll be stuck doing checklist-level work indefinitely if he never gets exposure to the harder assessments.

Motivations

  • Wants to grow into more technical, judgment-based work like Priya's.
  • Eager to prove reliability early in his career — treats missed SLAs and re-keying errors as reputational, not just operational, problems.
  • Values structure and process, but wants that structure paired with increasing responsibility over time, not a ceiling.
  • Wants to reduce his own error rate as a way of building credibility with Dana and Priya.

Tools he uses today (pre-Tessera)

  • Shared Excel/Google Sheets vendor tracking register.
  • Email templates for chasing vendor responses.
  • Legacy GRC/ticketing tool for workflow status.
  • A basic intake form (SharePoint list) that dumps into manual review.
  • PDF viewer for reading questionnaire submissions before re-keying them.

Compliance frameworks Marcus works within

  • NYDFS 23 NYCRR 500, GLBA Safeguards Rule, US interagency TPRM guidance — primary frameworks for the US-side vendors he screens.
  • EU AI Act (initial screening only) — flags potential AI-vendor relevance up to Priya/Dana rather than making the determination himself.
  • SOC 2 Type II — receives and files these, but doesn't yet do the deep technical read Priya does.

Full detail in compliance/README.md.

How Tessera helps Marcus

  • Automatically ingests and parses standard questionnaire submissions, eliminating manual re-keying into a spreadsheet.
  • Auto-triages and routes ambiguous or higher-complexity vendors up to Priya or Dana, instead of leaving that judgment call entirely on him.
  • Gives him a single prioritized queue with real SLA countdowns, instead of tracking deadlines by memory and spreadsheet.
  • Frees up time for him to shadow Priya's deeper reviews and build toward more technical assessment work.
  • Keeps a clear, attributable record of his work product — useful for his own performance review and case for growth.
