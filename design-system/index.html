Known contrast gaps, found while documenting. The status colours are fills, not text colours. Where the UI uses them as small text, two fall just under WCAG AA (4.5:1): critical red on white (4.4:1, and 3.9:1 on the canvas) and warning orange (4.3:1). In the task age bar, white numbers on the orange and green segments measure 2.7:1 and 3.0:1. The nav group labels (#5A6C8A on the nav) are 3.3:1. Recommended fixes: critical text #B53A3A (5.8:1 on white, 5.2:1 on canvas), warning text #A85E1A (4.9:1), ink numbers on the orange and green age segments. Status is never colour alone: every tag carries an icon or a word.

The faded card

Every card surface fades in from the page background: a transparent-to-white gradient, borders on the sides and bottom only, and rounded bottom corners. It has no top edge, so it reads as rising out of the page. Cards taller than 143px fade over the first 143px and are solid below, so table rows stay legible. Source: card.md, Figma node 19:1232.

AI patterns: Tessera AI chat

Every dashboard card has a sparkle that opens a chat about that card. It starts by asking what you want to learn or act on, shows the card's figures, and never acts without approval. This follows the human-oversight and explainability expectations in the compliance notes.

Rules this pattern follows
  • Scoped: the chat opens with the card's own figures, so the question has a subject.
  • Propose, then approve: an action shows exactly what will change and waits for a click. "Not now" changes nothing.
  • Explainable: the score, a call and an action each say why, in plain words and with the numbers behind them.
  • Overrides need a reason, and the reason is kept for the audit record.
  • Honest about limits: the footer says it can be wrong, and an unknown question gets "I can't answer that yet" plus what it can answer.
