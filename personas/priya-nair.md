Priya is the team's technical depth: she owns deep-dive assessments of Northbridge's most critical and complex vendors, especially those touching EU operations and subject to DORA and NIS2.

## Goals

- Be the team's go-to for technical security evidence review — the person Dana and Marcus escalate hard calls to.
- Get Northbridge's EU critical-ICT-vendor register (DORA) to genuinely reflect reality, not just pass a point-in-time audit.
- Write risk findings clearly enough the first time that she isn't re-explaining them to Legal or Dana after the fact.
- Build a track record that supports moving toward a security-leadership or Director-level TPRM role.

## A day in the life

- **9:00 am** (London) — Triages new critical-vendor submissions that came in overnight from the US/Canada teams.
- **10:00 am** — Deep-reviews a SOC 2 Type II report and a penetration test summary for a vendor providing AI-based fraud detection — cross-checking claimed controls against actual test results.
- **11:30 am** — Escalation call with the vendor's security team about an exception she found buried on page 47 of their SOC 2 report.
- **1:00 pm** — Documents the finding in the risk register, decides how the vendor should be tiered under DORA's critical-ICT-provider criteria — criteria she has to interpret herself, since internal guidance hasn't caught up to the regulation.
- **3:00 pm** — Syncs with Dana on a vendor flagged as possibly "high-risk" under the EU AI Act.
- **4:30 pm** — Tries to make a dent in a backlog of routine reviews that keep getting bumped by urgent critical-vendor work.

## Responsibilities

- Owns technical deep-dive assessments for critical and high-complexity vendors.
- Reviews security evidence: SOC 2 reports, penetration test results, vulnerability scans, architecture/security questionnaires.
- Applies DORA and NIS2 criticality-tiering logic to EU-relevant vendors.
- Screens vendors with embedded AI features for EU AI Act relevance, in coordination with Dana and Legal.
- Mentors Marcus on the technical side of assessments as he grows into more complex work.

## Pain points

- **Evidence volume** — manually reading 60+ page SOC 2 reports and pen test summaries per vendor, hunting for exceptions that are often buried deep in an appendix.
- **No memory across vendors** — when a vendor reappears (renewal, new subsidiary), she often has to redo analysis from scratch because past findings aren't easily searchable or linked.
- **Regulatory ambiguity, personal risk** — DORA's "critical ICT third-party" criteria are still maturing; she's the one making judgment calls with real regulatory consequences and limited internal precedent to lean on.
- **Constant context-switching** — jumping between EU-specific (DORA/NIS2) and general assessments breaks her focus.
- **Escalation bottleneck** — she's the only technical escalation point, so her backlog of routine reviews grows every time a critical vendor needs her attention.

## Motivations

- Wants to be recognized for technical judgment, not just checklist completion.
- Takes real pride in catching what a surface-level read would miss — treats it like detective work.
- Cares about getting the call right, not just getting it done fast — the downside of a wrong tiering decision is real.
- Long-term: wants a path toward security-leadership or Director-level TPRM work.

## Representative quote

## How Tessera helps Priya

- AI pre-reads SOC 2 reports, pen test summaries, and questionnaires, surfacing exceptions and flagged findings with direct page references instead of requiring a full manual read-through.
- Keeps a searchable history of past findings per vendor, so renewals and repeat vendors don't mean starting from zero.
- Built-in DORA/NIS2 criticality-tiering logic gives her a consistent starting point for ambiguous calls, which she can then confirm or override — with her override reasoning captured for audit purposes.
- Frees her time from document triage so more of her day goes to the technical judgment calls only she can make, and to mentoring Marcus.
